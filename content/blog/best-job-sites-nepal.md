## Introduction

Choosing a job site is less about finding one “best” website and more about using the right source for the role you want. A graduate looking for an internship, a software engineer searching for remote work, and a candidate preparing for a public-service vacancy may need different sources.

This guide explains how to compare job sites in Nepal, how to use an aggregator such as KamKhoj, and how to verify a listing before you apply.

## What makes a job site useful?

Look for five things:

1. **Freshness:** Does the listing show a publication or closing date?
2. **Relevance:** Can you filter by location, category, experience, and work type?
3. **Detail:** Does the listing explain responsibilities, requirements, and how to apply?
4. **Source clarity:** Can you identify the employer and original application page?
5. **Search discipline:** Can you quickly remove expired, duplicate, or irrelevant results?

No platform gets every one of these right for every industry. Compare the experience for your specific search instead of trusting a ranking copied from another article.

## Popular job-search options in Nepal

### MeroJob

MeroJob is commonly used for corporate, technology, development, and graduate roles. It is useful when you want a large selection and are willing to create a profile or complete an application on the source site.

Before applying, check whether the role is still open and whether the employer has updated the closing date. A listing appearing in search results is not, by itself, proof that applications are still being accepted.

### JobsNepal

JobsNepal is useful for professional, banking, development, education, and general vacancies. Read the full notice carefully because some roles have document, experience, or submission requirements that are easy to miss in a short card view.

### KumariJob

KumariJob can be useful for banking, finance, sales, administration, and other traditional sectors. Use the location and job-type filters, then compare the employer’s requirements with your actual experience before spending time on an application.

### Specialist and source-specific sites

Some industries and communities publish roles through specialist portals, company career pages, LinkedIn, professional groups, or direct referrals. A smaller source may have fewer listings but more context for a particular field.

## A reliable job-search workflow

### Step 1: Start with a specific search

Search for a role, skill, or industry rather than only “jobs in Nepal.” Examples include “junior accountant,” “React developer,” “marketing intern,” or “customer support Kathmandu.”

### Step 2: Compare the important facts

For each promising result, note:

- Employer name
- Location and work arrangement
- Required experience
- Closing date
- Salary information, if provided
- Original source

### Step 3: Open the original listing

Read the complete notice. Check the source’s date, application instructions, required documents, and contact information. If the source page is closed or gives different information, treat the source page as current.

### Step 4: Tailor your application

Adjust your CV and short message to the role. Highlight evidence—projects, outcomes, tools, certificates, or responsibilities—instead of copying every keyword into a profile.

### Step 5: Keep a simple application record

Record the employer, role, application date, source URL, follow-up date, and status. This prevents duplicate applications and makes follow-up more professional.

## How to spot a risky listing

Be cautious when a listing:

- Requests money for an interview, registration, or training.
- Uses a personal email while claiming to represent a large employer.
- Has no clear employer name or responsibilities.
- Promises unusually high income for very little work.
- Asks for sensitive identity or banking documents before a legitimate hiring stage.
- Appears expired on the original source.
