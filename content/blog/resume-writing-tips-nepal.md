## Introduction

A resume does not get hired on its own, but it decides whether an employer spends another thirty seconds on you or moves on. In Nepal, many candidates lose that chance because their resume is too generic, too long, or too vague about what they actually did.

The goal of a good resume is simple: help the reader understand your fit quickly. Whether you are applying for an internship, a bank role, a marketing position, or a software job, your CV should make it easy to answer three questions:

- What kind of work have you done?
- What can you do for this role?
- Is your experience relevant enough to interview you?

## What Employers Usually Notice First

Most hiring teams do not read a resume from top to bottom on the first pass. They scan for a few signals:

- A clear headline or summary
- Relevant job titles or project work
- Skills that match the vacancy
- Dates that make sense
- Obvious communication mistakes

This is why clutter hurts. If your strongest qualification is buried under unnecessary personal details, school achievements from years ago, or a long paragraph of generic adjectives, your best information gets missed.

## A Simple Resume Structure That Works

For most Nepal job applications, a one-page or two-page resume is enough. A clear structure usually works better than a decorative template.

### 1. Contact Details

Include:

- Full name
- Phone number
- Professional email address
- City or district
- LinkedIn profile if it is active and complete
- Portfolio or GitHub link if the role benefits from it

You usually do not need to include your full home address. Only add personal details that are useful for the employer's next step.

### 2. Short Professional Summary

Keep this to two or three lines. It should describe your level, focus, and strongest value.

Examples:

- "Marketing graduate with internship experience in social media planning, content scheduling, and campaign reporting."
- "Junior React developer with two freelance projects and experience building responsive dashboards and landing pages."

Avoid lines such as "hardworking, honest, sincere, and dedicated individual seeking a challenging opportunity." They are common, but they do not tell the employer anything concrete.

### 3. Experience or Project Work

If you already have work experience, list it in reverse chronological order. For each role, include:

- Company name
- Role title
- Dates
- Two to four bullet points about outcomes, not just duties

Instead of:

- "Responsible for social media"

Write:

- "Planned weekly social media calendar and coordinated with design on campaign assets"
- "Tracked post performance and prepared monthly reporting for the marketing lead"

If you are a fresher, use projects, internships, volunteer work, college responsibilities, or freelance assignments. Real proof matters more than trying to sound senior.

### 4. Education

List your latest degree or current study status first. If you are early in your career, education can appear above experience. If you already have solid work history, it can go lower.

Useful education details:

- Degree and institution
- Graduation year or expected completion date
- Relevant coursework only if it truly supports the role

### 5. Skills

Group skills in a readable way instead of making one long list.

Examples:

- Tools: Excel, Canva, Google Sheets, Power BI
- Languages: Nepali, English, Hindi
- Technical: React, TypeScript, Node.js, PostgreSQL

Only include skills you can defend in an interview.

## How to Tailor a Resume for Nepal Jobs

Tailoring does not mean rewriting everything. It means adjusting the parts that should reflect the job you want.

Check the vacancy and highlight:

- The main job title
- Tools or software mentioned
- Required communication or client-facing work
- Industry-specific keywords

Then update your summary, key bullets, and skill order. If you are applying for a bank operations role and a sales role on the same day, those resumes should not look identical.

## What Freshers Should Add When Experience Is Limited

Freshers often think they have "nothing to write." Usually that is not true. Add evidence of responsibility and learning, such as:

- Internship tasks
- Final-year projects
- Event coordination
- Student leadership
- Volunteer work
- Freelance or family business support
- Coursework tied to the job

The key is to explain what you handled, what tools you used, and what result came from it.

## Common Resume Mistakes

These issues appear often and reduce credibility fast:

- Using the same CV for every role
- Spelling company names or tools incorrectly
- Writing long paragraphs instead of readable bullets
- Listing too many weak or outdated skills
- Adding a photo when the rest of the resume is still unclear
- Including salary expectations on the resume unless specifically asked

Another common mistake is copying resume language from the internet. If the wording sounds unnatural, recruiters notice.

## Final Resume Checklist

Before you send your application, check:

- Is the target role obvious in the first section?
- Are your best two or three qualifications easy to find?
- Do your bullets show actions and results?
- Have you removed information that does not help?
- Did you save the file with a professional name such as `FirstName-LastName-Resume.pdf`?

## Conclusion

A good Nepal resume is not about sounding impressive everywhere. It is about being clear, relevant, and easy to trust. If you focus on proof, readable structure, and role-specific editing, your application gets much stronger even before the interview starts.
